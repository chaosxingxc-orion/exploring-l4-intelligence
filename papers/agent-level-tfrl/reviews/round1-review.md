## Area-chair meta-review

**Verdict: major revision** — mean soundness 3, mean contribution 3.


A proposal paper (theory + feasibility + plan + preliminary results) arguing training-free RL (Gibbs tilting q* ∝ q0 exp(R/β)) has vanishing single-model gain at small reward spread (OSA-1), recovers additively under context-isolated agents (OSA-2), and is reachable only under credit assignment plus a β-KL trust region (OSA-3); instantiated as a frozen two-omni speech agent. Strong cross-reviewer consensus: (1) the STATIC tilting algebra is correct and competently Lean-formalized — verified on disk (toolchain/mathlib pins match Appendix A, exactly one documented sorry, no axioms/admit, theorem names match), with genuinely above-average honesty infrastructure (rem:honesty, the §6 convergence table, the §7.3 novelty delta, the §10 threat enumeration, and a model §9.5 pre-registration); but (2) the PROSE over-claims relative to the theorems in load-bearing, partly self-contradictory ways. The decisive issue — the prompt's "does OSA support the pivot" — is that OSA-2 (Thm gain_product) proves only additivity GIVEN a product base with non-degenerate separable per-component rewards; it never proves that lifting a fixed flat single-model task into an agent converts a flat reward into non-flat blocks, nor lower-bounds per-block spread. As four reviewers independently note, "single-model inert → agent recovers" is, in the formalism, identical to "flat reward → high-spread reward," and the paper's own Table 3 (single-model Operator-B best-of-N, +0.33) shows the high-spread case is reachable WITHOUT any agent — contradicting the abstract's "asymptotically inert on a single frozen model." Compounding this, the flagship system's central credit signal (§8.2 dual-use key-agreement, "no human label and no learned judge required") contradicts the paper's own Definition 2.6 of a verifiable reward, since the keys are themselves frozen-model (x-vector/ECAPA/SER) estimates — self-consistency, not ground-truth-derived. Convergence is asserted as established fact in §8.3/§8.4 ("the external state trajectory converges") and analogized in the §5 hinge ("OSA-3a's non-convergence becomes OSA-3b's convergence"), yet §6 and §10 correctly call finite-time convergence OPEN; §5/§8 must be brought into line with that honesty. The "machine-checked in Lean 4" headline over-credits: the qualitative OSA core is sorry-free, but the quantitative bounds (spread²/8β via assumed Hoeffding, the best-of-N KL bound carrying the one sorry, the √log N regret via assumed Pinsker) are Lean theorems CONDITIONAL on named hypotheses — disclosed in rem:honesty but not in the abstract/C1. Citation integrity is a real risk: several load-bearing 2026 preprints carry precise theorem/statistic attributions (jitrl2026 "realizes exactly the KL-constrained optimal policy"; skillsbench2026 "+16.2pp, 16/84 negative") that no reviewer could verify against source. Empirically, the preliminary numbers fall below the paper's own §9.5 bar (single seed, no paired-delta CI on the load-bearing +0.097, a 74-config winner's-curse sweep, emotion double-counted across the Op-A and Op-B tables, untraceable to committed artifacts, an Op-A row mislabeled Op-B, unsupported "cross-team" provenance, no contamination guard), and speech-specific assumptions are internally inconsistent (the content-contrastive memory encoder is at chance on speaker and weak on emotion — the very factors the paralinguistic benchmark needs). None of this is fatal for a proposal: the proven mathematics is correct and the fixes are reframing + one new result (a per-block spread floor) + retroactively applying §9.5 discipline + citation verification, not retraction. Mean soundness 3.0, mean contribution 3.0; clear MAJOR REVISION (4 of 5 reviewers major, 1 minor).


### Must-fix (prioritized)


**[1][critical]** _§5.3 Theorem gain_product (thm:gainprod); Abstract; Intro OSA-2 paragraph (sec:intro)_ — OSA-2 does not prove the advertised agentic recovery / 'the pivot.' gain_product establishes only that gain is ADDITIVE given a product base with NON-DEGENERATE additively-separable per-component rewards. OSA-1's collapse is about a FLAT reward; OSA-2 silently switches to non-flat separable components, so the trichotomy compares different reward functions. It never proves that decomposing a fixed entangled single-model task into an agent converts a flat reward into non-degenerate blocks, nor that per-block spread is bounded away from zero — if each isolated context is served by the same frozen model with the same flat conditioning, the sum of k near-zero gains is still near-zero, so 'growing in k' is vacuous. The causal gloss 'Isolation is what restores spread: separate contexts do not average one another's rewards toward the flat band' is formalized nowhere. This is the central 'does the theory support the agent pivot' failure.
  
  *Fix:* Either (a) add a theorem giving a LOWER bound on per-block spread/gain under the actual frozen-model context-isolation construction, so the k-fold sum is provably bounded away from the single-model ceiling; or (b) explicitly downgrade OSA-2 to 'additivity of the objective under an ASSUMED non-degenerate per-block reward' and move the empirical crux (that isolation yields non-flat blocks) to a Phase-2 conjecture. ## Individual reviews


### Theory Critic (Section 5 OSA mathematics + appendix)

*Recommendation: major revision — soundness 3/5, contribution 3/5.*


**Summary.** The paper casts training-free RL as Gibbs tilting q* ∝ q0 exp(R/β) of a frozen base policy and proves an "optimization-space adequacy" trichotomy: OSA-1 (single-model gain vanishes as reward spread → 0, bounded by spread²/(8β) via Hoeffding), OSA-2 (gain becomes additive and grows in k under context-isolated product structure), OSA-3 (per-component tilting reaches the global optimum but a naive rollout retains a strictly positive KL deficit, motivating a β-KL trust region). Core identities are formalized in Lean 4 with one documented sorry (best-of-N order statistics) and Hoeffding isolated as a named hypothesis; the theory is instantiated as a frozen two-omni speech agent with a verifiable-reward gate. The static algebra is correct, but several load-bearing narrative claims (agentic "recovery," trust-region convergence) exceed what the theorems actually establish.


**Strengths.**

- The master identity F(q) = β log Z − β·KL(q‖q*) (Lemma, sec:osa-tilt) and its consequences — T1 tilting optimality, gain_eq, gain_nonneg, flat_no_gain, Zpart_product, gain_product, qstar_product, rollout_deficit — are stated and proved correctly; the algebra is clean and the Gibbs optimum / closed-form value β log Z is right.
- Honest, explicit disclosure of formalization limits: Remark (Honesty about the formalization, rem:honesty) names both the single documented Lean sorry (klBoN_le_klBoundBoN, order statistics) and the isolated Hoeffding hypothesis (gain_le_of_hoeffding), and the appendix reproduces the Lean signatures so a reader can see exactly what is and isn't discharged.
- The convergence section (§6, Table 1) is unusually candid, correctly separating proven finite-N output-level guarantees from the merely-asymptotic, drift-conditioned JitRL result and the non-transferable UCT/LATS guarantees, and explicitly naming the finite-time monotone guarantee over Z as an OPEN problem.
- The Hoeffding ceiling spread²/(8β) is derived correctly in Appendix Lemma (cgf φ''≤(b−a)²/4 Popoviciu bound, λ=1), and the qualitative OSA-1 core (gain_eq/gain_nonneg/flat_no_gain) is genuinely sorry-free.
- Positioning is candid: the agent mechanism is explicitly disclaimed as non-novel, with the contribution scoped to domain transfer plus the optimization-space theory.


**Weaknesses.**

- **[major]** (Theorem (gain is additive over isolated components, thm:gainprod, sec:osa2); Intro OSA-2 paragraph (sec:intro, 'Isolation is what restores spread'); Appendix Corollary (Additive gain, app:product)) OSA-2 does not prove the advertised 'recovery.' Theorem gain_product (sec:osa2) shows only that IF the action space is a product q0 = q0^(1)⊗q0^(2) with an ADDITIVELY SEPARABLE reward R = R1+R2 whose components are non-constant, THEN gain is additive. But OSA-1's collapse is about a FLAT reward (spread→0); OSA-2 assumes NON-flat separable components. These are different reward functions, so the trichotomy compares apples to oranges. Critically, if the single-model base were ALSO a product with separable reward, gain_mono would already equal gain_1+gain_2 — isolation buys nothing. The genuine claim ('isolation restores spread that single-model entanglement destroyed') would require gain(isolated) ≥ gain(entangled) for the SAME task; that is never proved. But Phase 1 construction (Sec 9, sub:phase1, item v) builds the benchmark by re-keying an existing corpus with 'a frozen SER labeler' (a learned scorer), human-checking only a subset. At scale the SER 'ground truth' is therefore a learned model's output, so SER-keyed rewards and SER-keyed queries are agreement-with-a-model, i.e. model-judged in disguise and circular, not verifiable. This directly contradicts def:verifiable and undermines the central 'verifiable speech rewards' claim for the paralinguistic axis.  
  *Fix:* Either (a) restrict SER ground truth to human-annotated labels for the entire evaluated set (not a subset) so the reward is genuinely label-derived, or (b) reclassify SER as a surrogate/model-judged reward and stop counting it under the verifiable-reward guarantees; report inter-annotator agreement for SER labels and propagate that uncertainty into the gain estimate. Speaker-ID can remain verifiable where corpus speaker labels exist.
- **[major]** (Sec 8 subsec:memory (Index on audio + Retrieve-rerank) vs Sec 7.4 Tables tab:cremad-matrix, tab:opA) Internal tension between the chosen memory encoder and the paralinguistic retrieval goal. The system indexes on the frozen vector-omni audio embedding (Sec 8, 'Index on audio, not transcripts') and retrieves by late-interaction in Z_A. But the paper's own preliminary results (Sec 7.4, Tables 1-2) show that same omni-embed-nemotron-3b embedding is at chance on speaker (0.04, every layer) and only ~0.36-0.49 on emotion. A content-contrastive embedder that discards speaker/affect (exactly as the paper's related work argues, Sec 3.3 xiao2020/wang2020) cannot support paralinguistically-discriminative cross-session retrieval. So the dense retrieval key is blind to precisely the factors the benchmark requires.  
  *Fix:* Decouple the dense retrieval key from the paralinguistic key explicitly: retrieve on dedicated speaker (ECAPA/x-vector) and SER embeddings for paralinguistic queries, and reserve the omni content embedding for content retrieval. State which embedding answers which query type, and show (even in preliminary form) that retrieval recall@k on speaker/emotion queries is above chance with the chosen key.
- **[major]** (Sec 7.4 (Operator-B paragraph, tab:opB) and the framing in Sec 1 / Sec 9 sub:phase1) The evidence base for the headline application (compounding PARALINGUISTIC competence) is the weakest part of the results. The large Operator-B gains in Table 3 (SLURP +0.330, MInDS-14 +0.132, URO spoken-QA +0.335) are all CONTENT/intent tasks; the only paralinguistic gain (emotion +0.097) has touching CIs and SER absolute accuracy is ~0.49, with speaker at chance. The paper repeatedly motivates the agent by paralinguistic memory, but its strongest numbers do not bear on paralinguistics.  
  *Fix:* Either reframe the flagship application around content/intent compounding (where the evidence is strong) or add preliminary paralinguistic-compounding evidence. Be explicit that current SER/speaker perception (~0.49 / chance) may be too weak to support reliable affect-keyed rewards, and tie the Phase 2 success criterion for SER to a minimum perception floor.
- **[major]** (Sec 9 sub:phase1 (Minimal spec items i-ii, Construction item v)) Benchmark construction is acoustically unrealistic for a cross-session memory test. Re-keying single-speaker clip corpora (CREMA-D, SLURP/MInDS-14) into 'multi-session spoken dialogues with >=3 sessions per persona' yields synthetic continuity: the same clean, channel-matched audio is reused across 'sessions', so cross-session speaker linking is the trivial corpus label rather than open-set verification under device/time/channel mismatch, and diarization (pyannote) is unnecessary on single-speaker clips. Real cross-session paralinguistic memory must contend with DER (often 10-30% on conversational audio) and intra-speaker affect/channel drift, which this construction removes.  
  *Fix:* Introduce genuine cross-session variation (different recording conditions/sessions per speaker, or a corpus with real repeated-caller structure such as call-center/longitudinal data), measure and report diarization DER and SER error on the constructed set, and demonstrate that queries cannot be solved by exact-audio matching. Otherwise the benchmark overstates difficulty and will not stress the error regimes the system claims to handle.
- **[major]** (Sec 8 subsec:memory (Dual-use composite keys) and Sec 10 (dual-use paragraph)) Speaker-ID and SER keys/rewards are treated symmetrically despite very different error profiles. Modern speaker embeddings (ECAPA/x-vector) are reliable on clean enrolled speech (EER ~1-2%), but SER error is large (state-of-the-art ~0.7-0.8 on acted corpora, far worse in the wild; the paper's own omni reads ~0.49) and emotion labels are subjective with low inter-annotator agreement. A composite key k=(speaker-ID, emotion/SER, turn-index) inherits the weakest field, and the dual-use design means an SER error both mis-routes and mis-rewards (acknowledged in Sec 10 but only as future work).  
  *Fix:* Separate the reliability budget per key field: gate SER-keyed memory writes/rewards behind an SER-confidence threshold or abstention, report SER calibration, and weight the composite key by per-field reliability. Do not present SER and speaker-ID as interchangeable verifiable signals.
- **[major]** (Sec 9 sub:phase1 (Queries item ii) and Sec 10 (read-not-listen paragraph)) Read-not-listen is addressed only at the query-design level and is not validated; the policy itself is documented to read-not-listen. The benchmark asserts queries 'can only be answered by retaining who spoke and how they sounded,' but provides no construction-time check that transcript content does not leak the answer, and the generative omni policy the paper relies on is exactly the kind shown to answer paralinguistic questions from text priors (voxparadox2026, listenortranscribe2025, cited in Sec 3.2).  
  *Fix:* Add an explicit text-only / transcript-ablation control: run a strong text LLM on transcripts alone and require it to score at chance on the paralinguistic queries before a query is admitted (a listen-only filter). Report the gap between audio-input and transcript-only performance as a primary benchmark metric.
- **[minor]** (Sec 7.4 ('Reading the evidence' and tab:cremad-matrix interpretation)) Using the content-embedder's speaker-blindness as empirical support for OSA-1 'flat conditioning' conflates two different phenomena. The speaker-at-chance result (Sec 7.4) is the expected by-design behavior of a contrastive content embedder that discards speaker (the paper itself argues this in Sec 3.3), not evidence that the single-model reward-spread is generically small. The OSA-1 'flatness' reading is overstated for factors the encoder was trained to remove.  
  *Fix:* Qualify the OSA-1 empirical claim: distinguish 'flat because the factor is genuinely low-spread under q0' from 'flat because this particular encoder removed the factor.' Show flatness on a factor the encoder is expected to retain (content/intent) to make the OSA-1 point without the confound.
- **[minor]** (Sec 8 subsec:memory (Index on audio) and Sec 9 sub:phase2/sub:phase3) No transcript-indexed memory baseline is planned, yet 'index on audio not transcript' is a load-bearing design claim. Phase 2/3 compare against single-shot best-of-N with empty memory, but never against an ASR-transcript-keyed memory, so the central audio-indexing thesis is asserted, not tested.  
  *Fix:* Add a transcript-indexed memory ablation (ASR -> text embedding -> retrieve) as a baseline, and report the paralinguistic-query gap between audio-keyed and transcript-keyed memory to substantiate the design choice.
- **[minor]** (Sec 3.7 (rw-speech-agents) and Sec 9 sub:phase0/sub:protocol) URO-Bench, VoiceBench, and AudioMC are positioned as the relevant prior single-episode benchmarks but are not actually used as policy-quality anchors in the plan. Phase 0 anchors only on SER ~0.49 and intent ~0.25; the agent's general spoken competence is never validated on the established suites it cites.  
  *Fix:* Add a one-time evaluation of the frozen policy on at least VoiceBench and URO-Bench to establish that the backbone is competitive before measuring compounding, so cross-session gains are not confounded by a weak base policy.

### Novelty & Reward-Hacking Red-Teamer

*Recommendation: major revision — soundness 2/5, contribution 2/5.*


**Summary.** A research proposal arguing that training-free RL (Gibbs tilting q* proportional to q0 exp(R/beta), no weight update) has a vanishing optimization gain on a single frozen model when reward spread is small (OSA-1, Hoeffding bound G <= spread^2/8beta, Lean-checked), recovers additively when the model is lifted into a context-isolated agent system (OSA-2, gain is a sum over independent reward blocks), and is reachable only under credit assignment plus a beta-KL trust region (OSA-3). It instantiates this as a self-evolving omni speech agent (frozen vector-omni memory, generative-omni policy, verifiable-reward acceptance gate) and reports preliminary frozen-embedding probe results plus a staged plan. The authors are explicit that the agent mechanism is not novel; they frame the contribution as domain transfer plus two-omni pairing plus verifiable speech rewards plus the OSA theory, with the theory billed as the load-bearing contribution.


**Strengths.**

- Unusually honest positioning of the mechanism. The intro paragraph 'What is and is not novel', the 7.3 Novelty Delta, and the section 10 paragraphs 'The mechanism is not novel; it is a domain transfer' and 'The moat is an absence of evidence' perform much of the hedging a red-teamer would otherwise demand. rem:honesty (section 5) and section 10 disclose the two Lean gaps (assumed Hoeffding, T2 sorry) rather than hiding them.
- Reward hacking / Goodhart is treated more thoroughly than is typical for a proposal: Assumption 2.7 (verifiable-only rewards), the 2.4 argument that the tilt sharpens along R so misspecification is amplified as beta->0, and three dedicated section 10 paragraphs (WER gaming by hedging/copying, dual-use paralinguistic keys as a positive-feedback pathway, acoustic memory poisoning) show genuine adversarial self-examination.
- The individual proven theorems (master identity Lemma, T1 tilting optimality, gain closed form, flat-no-gain, gain_product) are correct, and the 9.5 evaluation protocol (verifiable-only rewards in every selection path, locked test touched once, pre-registered kill criteria, paired bootstrap, family-wise correction, true few-shot hygiene) is disciplined and falsifiable.
- The preliminary tables (7.4) are reported with CIs and the CI overlaps on the weak emotion effects are stated explicitly rather than buried.


**Weaknesses.**

- **[critical]** (Theorem gain_product / OSA-2 (section 5.3, thm:gainprod); intro thesis 'agentic recovery'; abstract 'RECOVERS additively ... growing in the number of isolated contexts') The central 'single-model collapse -> agentic recovery' narrative is, in the formalism, a reframing of 'small reward spread -> large reward spread' and does not actually establish that an AGENT SYSTEM escapes OSA-1. OSA-2 (Thm gain_product) only proves the tautology that the log-partition of a product measure is additive: IF you supply K independent blocks each with a NON-DEGENERATE reward, the total gain is the sum and grows in K. But OSA-1's whole premise is that on the frozen model the realizable reward spread is small (flat conditioning). OSA-2 assumes each isolated context has its own non-flat reward, i.e. it assumes away exactly the flatness OSA-1 establishes. If each isolated context is served by the SAME frozen model with the SAME flat conditioning, each gain(R_i) ~ 0 and the sum of K near-zeros is still ~0; 'growing in k' is vacuous unless per-block spread is bounded away from 0, which is never proven. The recovery hinges entirely on an unproven empirical premise that is in direct tension with the paper's own OSA-1 finding.  
  *Fix:* Stop presenting OSA-2 as the load-bearing recovery theorem. Either (a) add a theorem giving a LOWER bound on per-block spread (or per-block gain) under context isolation for the actual frozen-model construction, so that sum over K is provably bounded away from the single-model ceiling, or (b) explicitly downgrade OSA-2 to 'additivity of the objective under an ASSUMED non-degenerate per-block reward' and move the real claim (that isolation yields non-flat blocks) to an empirical conjecture to be tested in Phase 2/3. Delete the unsupported causal gloss 'Isolation is what restores spread: separate contexts do not average one another's rewards toward the flat band' (intro) -- nothing in gain_product proves any 'un-averaging'.
- **[critical]** (Abstract ('asymptotically inert (OSA-1)'); intro OSA-1 paragraph; Table opB and 'Operator-B task-level selection already yields large gains' (7.4); C3) The preliminary results offered as 'early agentic recovery' (C3) are NOT agent-system evidence and they CONTRADICT the abstract's claim that single-model TFRL is 'asymptotically inert'. Table opB (7.4) reports single-model Operator-B best-of-N selection: SLURP intent +0.330, URO spoken-QA +0.335, MInDS-14 +0.132. These are single frozen models, no memory, no skills, no multi-context decomposition -- pure single-model training-free RL with a high-spread verifiable reward.
