\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:threats}

We hold this work to the standard of self-criticism it advocates, and enumerate the threats in
descending order of how much they could change the paper's claims. The scope is deliberately narrow:
two \emph{frozen omni speech models}, each carrying exactly one of our two results, with no weight
update and no structural change. Our primary result is genuine TFRL: on a \emph{frozen
generative} omni model (Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B, Q8\_0 GGUF driven by \texttt{llama.cpp}
\citep{qwen3omni}) we sample $N$ candidate transcripts per utterance and \emph{select} with a
verifiable reward, i.e.\ best-of-$N$ over model-sampled candidates---which induces the order-statistics
selection distribution, heuristically (not provenly) linked to the Gibbs tilt at $\beta\to0$
(\Cref{sec:osa,app:lean}). Our secondary result is
\emph{probing}, not RL: a frozen content bi-encoder (\texttt{omni-embed-nemotron-3b}
\citep{omniembed2025}) is analysed for what paralinguistic information its read-out affords. These are
\emph{different frozen models} and \emph{different operators} --- a generative best-of-$N$ selector
versus a static embedding probe --- and we are careful throughout not to conflate them.

\paragraph{\texttt{llama.cpp} audio input is experimental upstream.}
The primary result runs the frozen omni model through \texttt{llama.cpp}, whose multimodal audio path
is flagged experimental upstream and documented as possibly reduced quality relative to the reference
runtime. Our greedy baseline corpus WER of $0.0973$ (snr5) / $0.0579$ (clean) on LibriSpeech test-other
is therefore a property of \emph{this} decoding path, and the absolute error rates --- though not
necessarily the \emph{relative} best-of-$N$ headroom, which is a within-runtime comparison --- may shift
under a higher-fidelity runtime. Every number is committed to \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_snr5.json}
and \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_clean.json} with the reproduce command, so the runtime dependence is
inspectable rather than hidden.

\paragraph{The noise condition changes the size of the headroom, and even the two conditions we test do not exhaust condition-space.}
We now evaluate \emph{both} LibriSpeech test-other with additive noise at SNR $5$\,dB and clean
test-other, precisely to test whether headroom survives outside the perturbation we originally chose to
expose it. It does: the oracle headroom is significant in both ($+0.0336$ snr5, $+0.0223$ clean at
$N{=}8$, corpus metric), smaller but not absent on clean, and the logprob-confidence deployable selector
clears significance in both as well. This narrows, but does not eliminate, the threat: only one noise
realization per utterance is drawn for snr5 (nested noise replicates are deferred), only one corpus
(LibriSpeech test-other) and one runtime (\texttt{llama.cpp}) are tested, and other noise types, SNR
levels, and corpora remain unmeasured. We report the two conditions we have, plainly, rather than
generalizing beyond them.

\paragraph{Among deployable selectors, only logprob-confidence currently clears significance, and only directionally.}
The honest core of the primary result is a \emph{realised-versus-headroom gap}, now partially narrowed.
The verifiable-reward argmax --- oracle-WER best-of-$N$, which induces the order-statistics selection
distribution heuristically linked to the reward-spread lens's $\beta\!\to\!0$ tilt --- reduces corpus WER
by $+0.0336$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0235,0.0453]$, significant, snr5) and $+0.0223$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0136,0.0316]$,
significant, clean) at $N{=}8$ (cohort seed $20260711$, noise seed $20260712$, $3$ independently-varied
pool seeds, $n{=}96$ fixed cohort, pool $8$, temperature $0.8$). But this oracle uses the reference
transcript to pick the best candidate, so it is an \emph{upper bound}, not a deployable procedure. Among
deployable, label-free selectors: MBR-consensus (corrected to a symmetric edit-distance implementation)
is positive but non-significant at $N{=}8$ in both conditions; \emph{logprob-confidence} is the sole
deployable selector whose corpus-WER CI excludes $0$ in both conditions ($+0.0081$ $[0.0005,0.0161]$
snr5, corpus-only; $+0.0094$ $[0.0034,0.0165]$ clean, both metrics), realizing roughly $24\%$/$42\%$ of
the respective oracle headroom. We flag this as \textbf{Stage-1 directional}, not a settled deployment
result: the snr5 CI lower bound is marginal, and no correction for multiple comparisons across selectors,
$N$, conditions, and metrics has been applied. The correct reading is thus threefold: the
verifiable-reward headroom is real and significant on a frozen model in both conditions (genuine TFRL);
logprob-confidence is the first deployable selector to clear significance in both, directionally; and MBR
still does not, on this benchmark, turn the headroom into a significant gain. We do not paper over the
remaining gap; closing it further is the main deployable open problem (\Cref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Possible pretraining contamination of the public sets.}
LibriSpeech, and any public set we touch, may overlap the pretraining corpora of the frozen models
\citep{contaminationsurvey2024,gorman2019splits,taskcontamination2024}, so absolute error rates can in
part reflect memorised material. Our primary claim is a \emph{within-utterance, within-runtime}
best-of-$N$ contrast --- how much a verifiable reward improves over the same model's greedy output on
the same items --- which is more robust to contamination than an absolute score. (Selection against a
\emph{proxy} reward can over-optimize regularities of the proxy \citep{gao2023overopt,skalse2022rewardhacking,spuriousrewards2025};
our oracle contrast uses the true WER objective, so this concern attaches to the deployable proxies
(MBR-consensus, logprob-confidence), not to the oracle headroom.) A
contamination-controlled split would still be needed to fully exclude leakage; until then the numbers
should be read as within-model, within-condition contrasts.

\paragraph{The C2 negative is specific to \emph{this} frozen content encoder, not to frozen models as a class.}
The secondary (probing) negative is precise and committed but narrowly scoped. On CREMA-D, a probe of
the frozen \texttt{omni-embed-nemotron-3b} \emph{content} read-out (dev-selected layer, three seeds,
\texttt{\_repro/paralinguistic\_negative\_probe.json}) sits near chance for speaker (mean accuracy
$0.033$, $3.0\times$ the $91$-way chance rate) and is only modest for emotion ($0.405$, $2.4\times$ the
$6$-way chance rate); and training-free pool-selection over that read-out yields \emph{no} significant
emotion gain (across-seed $95\%$ CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$, spanning zero,
\texttt{\_repro/emotion\_pool\_paired\_v2.json}). This bounds \emph{that content read-out} and nothing
more. A purpose-built speaker encoder such as an ECAPA-TDNN embedding \citep{ecapatdnn2020} would carry
speaker identity well --- a well-established property of dedicated speaker models that we \emph{cite}
rather than claim to have run. The finding is consistent with evidence that omni models tend to
``read, not listen'' on paralinguistic cues \citep{voxparadox2026,listenortranscribe2025} and that
speaker identity resists in-context correction \citep{spkverifllm2026}.

\paragraph{The MInDS result is a corrected factorial, not a zero-shot or reward-driven selection gain.}
The same frozen content encoder also does tool/intent classification via candidate-card cosine
retrieval on MInDS-14 en-US. The originally reported $+0.126$ ``policy'' gain
(\texttt{\_repro/minds14\_toolintent\_paired.json}) was a \textbf{transductive fixed 3-shot/class
support} condition --- held-out eval rows leaked into the card/support set --- confounding three factors
(instruction wording, schema, card content) at once, and is retracted as a zero-shot or causal-mechanism
claim (the raw accuracy numbers themselves are recomputable and not fabricated; only that framing is
invalid). A clean factorial with a disjoint card-pool/eval split and three disjoint support draws
(\texttt{\_repro/minds14\_toolintent\_v2.json}) separates instruction wording from card content: the
true zero-shot arm (instruction only, no examples) \emph{regresses} to $\mathrm{Acc@1}=0.467$ from a
$0.712$ naive baseline ($-0.245$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.286,-0.201]$, significant), while in-set example cards
alone drive $+0.246$, and the pure instruction-on-cards effect is only $+0.015$. Its ``verifiable
reward'' is merely the \emph{evaluation} metric and does \emph{not} drive any selection, so it is not
best-of-$N$, not Gibbs tilting, and not reward-driven RL --- and it is a \emph{distinct operator} from
the C1 generative best-of-$N$, with no claim of identical machinery between them. We therefore never
call this result zero-shot (beyond the one arm that is) or reward-guided-RL, and we drop the earlier
SLURP intent figure entirely, as it has no committed artifact.

\paragraph{Relation to best-of-$N$ / MBR / reranking (honest differentiation).}
The ingredients of the primary result --- best-of-$N$ and reranking
\citep{beirami2024bon,verdun2025softbon,gui2024bonbon}, MBR decoding and MBR-for-ASR
\citep{jinnai2024mbrbon,ichihara2025mbr,mbrasr2025}, controlled decoding
\citep{mudgal2024controlled}, test-time scaling \citep{snell2024ttscompute}, and the Gibbs-optimal
tilt $\qs\propto\qo\exp(\Rwd/\beta)$ from alignment-as-inference
\citep{korbak2022kl,yang2024asymptotics} --- are all public, and we claim none as novel. What is
specific here is applying a genuine verifiable-reward best-of-$N$ to a \emph{frozen omni generative
model} and reporting the honest realised-versus-headroom gap between the oracle upper bound and a
label-free deployable selector, alongside a committed paralinguistic \emph{probing} negative on a
second frozen model and the reward-spread lens that organises both. The contribution is integrative and
empirical, and should be weighted as such.

\paragraph{Where Goodhart applies --- and where it does not.}
Reward over-optimization is the exploitation of a \emph{gap} between a proxy and the true objective
\citep{gao2023overopt,skalse2022rewardhacking,rlvrincentive2025}. Our \emph{oracle} reward is WER against
the reference---it \emph{is} the true objective, so selecting the min-WER candidate is not reward-hacking
a proxy but simply picking the best transcript; there is no proxy--true gap for the argmax to exploit. Its
only defect is non-deployability: it consults the reference at selection time. Goodhart pressure applies
instead to the \emph{deployable} label-free selectors --- MBR-consensus and logprob-confidence (and to
any future learned reranker) --- where pushing $N$ can move mass toward whatever the proxy measures
rather than toward lower true WER; both proxies are reference-free precisely to be deployable, at the
cost of MBR's non-significant realized gain and logprob-confidence's still-modest, Stage-1-directional
one reported above. Because we change no weights, any such proxy failure is recoverable (lower $N$, better proxies).
The lens bounds all of this: no selector can exceed the oracle headroom, which is itself bounded by the
spread of the reward across the model's sampled candidates.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Two directions follow directly and honestly. First, \emph{stronger deployable selectors} --- learned
rerankers or reference-free quality estimators trained to approximate the verifiable reward --- to
close the remaining realised-versus-headroom gap; logprob-confidence is a first, Stage-1-directional
step past MBR-consensus, not a closed problem, and a powered, multiplicity-corrected replication is the
immediate next step (\Cref{sec:conclusion}). Second, \emph{larger and
clean-condition best-of-$N$} --- more candidates, clean audio, and additional corpora --- to map how
the oracle headroom varies with condition and $N$. A larger agent-level program (one frozen model
routing and rewarding another across sessions) remains deferred, not disproved: it would require a
cross-session, same-speaker corpus we do not have, and a genuinely new non-separable irreducibility
theorem showing that decomposing a task into isolated contexts can add optimization headroom a single
model lacks. Absent such a theorem, the spread lens is explicit that context isolation adds
\emph{nothing} on a fixed reward --- no new non-constant reward component, no new spread, and by the
identity below no new gain --- so that theorem is the precondition for any agent-level claim, and it is
exactly what our present data do not supply.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to test whether the gain available to TFRL --- inference-time selection under a
verifiable reward, changing no weights and no structure --- is real on frozen omni speech models, and
whether it is governed by the \emph{spread} of that reward across candidates. Our primary result
answers the first question affirmatively and modestly. On a frozen generative omni model
(Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B via \texttt{llama.cpp}) we sample $N$ transcripts per utterance on LibriSpeech
test-other, under both additive noise (snr5) and clean conditions, and select with a verifiable reward
--- genuine best-of-$N$ over model-sampled candidates, which induces the order-statistics selection
distribution over the pool (heuristically, not provenly, linked to the Gibbs tilt at $\beta\to0$;
\Cref{sec:osa,app:lean}). Using a seed-separated protocol (cohort seed $20260711$, noise seed
$20260712$, greedy temperature $0$, and $3$ independently-varied pool seeds), the verifiable-reward
argmax reduces corpus WER by $+0.0336$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0235,0.0453]$, significant, snr5, greedy
$0.0973$) and $+0.0223$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0136,0.0316]$, significant, clean, greedy $0.0579$) at $N{=}8$
(committed artifacts \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_snr5.json}, \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_clean.json}).
This is genuine TFRL: reward-driven selection over a frozen model's own samples, no weights touched.
Among deployable, label-free selectors, MBR-consensus (corrected to a symmetric edit-distance
implementation) realises a positive but non-significant fraction of that headroom in both conditions;
logprob-confidence is the sole deployable selector whose corpus-WER CI excludes $0$ in both conditions
($+0.0081$ $[0.0005,0.0161]$ snr5, corpus-only; $+0.0094$ $[0.0034,0.0165]$ clean, both metrics),
realising roughly $24\%$/$42\%$ of the oracle headroom --- a Stage-1 directional finding, not
multiplicity-corrected --- an honest realised-versus-headroom gap, now partially and provisionally
narrowed rather than a solved deployment.

Our secondary result is \emph{probing}, on a \emph{different} frozen model and via a \emph{different}
operator: a frozen content bi-encoder (\texttt{omni-embed-nemotron-3b}) is near chance for speaker
(mean $0.033$, $3.0\times$ the $91$-way chance rate) and only modest for emotion ($0.405$, $2.4\times$
the $6$-way chance rate) on CREMA-D, and TFRL pool-selection over its content read-out yields
no significant emotion gain (across-seed CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$). The same encoder does tool/intent
classification by candidate-card cosine retrieval on MInDS-14; once a clean factorial separates
instruction wording from card content, the true zero-shot arm (instruction only) \emph{regresses}
($\mathrm{Acc@1}=0.467$ vs.\ $0.712$ naive, $-0.245$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.286,-0.201]$, significant), and the
previously reported gain is attributable almost entirely to in-set example cards ($+0.246$), not to
instruction/policy wording ($+0.015$ on cards alone). This is a card-representation observation whose
reward is only the evaluation metric --- \emph{not} a reward-driven selection gain, never zero-shot
beyond the one arm that is, and not the same machinery as the generative best-of-$N$ above. We report
both the paralinguistic negative and the MInDS regression not as setbacks but as precise, committed
measurements that scope what this frozen \emph{content} read-out affords.

The lens that ties these together is deliberately lightweight and used qualitatively. The identity
$\gain=\beta\,\KL{\qo}{\qs}$ --- the free-energy / Donsker--Varadhan characterisation of the gap between
what the frozen model already does ($\qo$) and what the reward would have it do ($\qs$) --- is a known
result, not a novel theorem, and we invoke it to \emph{explain}, not to prove: the gain is bounded by
the reward spread over $\mathrm{supp}(\qo)$, so a flat reward yields vanishing gain while a high-spread
reward yields large gain, on the very same frozen model. Two strict, \texttt{sorry}-free Lean lemmas
anchor its endpoints: a flat reward forces zero gain (\texttt{flat\_no\_gain}), and a non-constant
reward forces strictly positive gain (\texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}). This is precisely the
contrast we observe. In the primary result the \emph{sign and ceiling} of the oracle best-of-$N$ headroom are governed by the
spread of the verifiable reward across the model's sampled candidates --- flat reward across candidates
would give no headroom, spread gives strictly-positive headroom bounded by that spread; its \emph{growth}
with $N$ is a distinct order-statistics effect of the order-statistics selection distribution the
hard-BoN oracle induces, and we do not claim a machine-proved link between that object and the Gibbs
tilt (\Cref{app:lean}). In the secondary result the
frozen content encoder exposes reward spread for content and intent but little for paralinguistics, so
selection over it has purchase in the former and essentially none in the latter.

The conclusion is therefore modest and, we believe, true. A verifiable-reward best-of-$N$ on a frozen
omni model yields significant, $N$-scaling headroom in both a noisy and a clean condition --- genuine
TFRL; among deployable selectors, logprob-confidence is the first to realise a significant, if modest and
Stage-1-directional, fraction of it, while MBR-consensus does not; a frozen content encoder is near
chance on paralinguistics and, once its zero-shot claim is properly isolated, regresses under true
zero-shot MInDS instruction; and the reward-spread lens explains the sign and ceiling of the first result
while remaining honest that the hard-BoN/Gibbs-tilt correspondence it leans on is a heuristic, not a
proof. What we offer is a small, reproducible set of results, internally re-verified by adversarial AI
agents (not an independent team), and a lens that predicts their sign --- not a deployable agentic
stack, and not a claim beyond the frozen models and committed artifacts we can currently stand behind.
