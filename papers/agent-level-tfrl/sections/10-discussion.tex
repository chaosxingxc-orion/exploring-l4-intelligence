\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:threats}

We hold this work to the standard of self-criticism it advocates, and enumerate the threats in
descending order of how much they could change the paper's claims. The scope is deliberately narrow:
a \emph{single frozen speech model}, with no weight update and no structural change, and
``training-free RL'' realised concretely as inference-time \emph{verifiable-reward selection} --- a
frozen bi-encoder (omni-embed-nemotron-3b, a SentenceTransformer) ranking candidate cards by cosine
under a verifiable reward. Every reproducible result below is of that one mechanism; we never ran a
local generative operator, and we make no claim about one. Our two load-bearing empirical findings are
a \emph{positive} selection gain where the reward has spread across candidates (MInDS-14 tool/intent,
\Cref{sec:feasibility}) and a \emph{negative} one where it does not (the paralinguistic read-out).

\paragraph{Possible pretraining contamination of the public content/intent sets.}
Our positive results are on public spoken-language-understanding corpora (MInDS-14 en-US, SLURP intent)
whose transcripts and label schemata may have entered the pretraining data of the frozen encoder. A
high Acc@1 on such sets can in part reflect memorised surface form rather than an activated latent
skill, and inference-time selection against a verifiable reward is, by construction, an
over-optimization engine that will happily exploit any such regularity
\citep{gao2023overopt,skalse2022rewardhacking,spuriousrewards2025}. We mitigate by reporting a
\emph{paired} contrast against a raw-schema baseline on the same items and by committing the artifact
(\texttt{\_repro/minds14\_toolintent\_paired.json}: Acc@1 $0.984$ vs.\ $0.857$, $\Delta=+0.126$, $95\%$
paired-bootstrap CI $[0.077,0.181]$, $n=182$, seed $42$), so the gain is measured as a within-item
improvement rather than an absolute score. But a contamination-controlled split
\citep{contaminationsurvey2024,gorman2019splits} would be needed to fully exclude leakage, and until
then the content/intent gains should be read as gains \emph{relative to the baseline read-out}, not as
evidence of uncontaminated generalisation.

\paragraph{The negative result is specific to \emph{this} frozen content encoder, not to frozen models as a class.}
Our negative is precise and committed, but narrowly scoped. On CREMA-D, a linear/kNN probe of the
frozen omni-embed \emph{content} read-out (dev-selected layer, three seeds,
\texttt{\_repro/paralinguistic\_negative\_probe.json}) sits near chance for speaker (mean accuracy
$0.033$, $3.0\times$ the $91$-way chance rate) and is only modest for emotion ($0.405$, $2.4\times$ the
$6$-way chance rate); and training-free pool-selection over that read-out yields \emph{no} significant
emotion gain (across-seed $95\%$ CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$, spanning zero,
\texttt{\_repro/emotion\_pool\_paired\_v2.json}). This bounds \emph{that read-out} and nothing more. A
purpose-built speaker encoder (e.g.\ an ECAPA-TDNN embedding) would carry speaker identity well; that
is a well-established property of dedicated speaker models, not something we tested here, and our claim
is only that the \emph{omni content read-out} affords little paralinguistic spread to select over. The
finding is thus a statement about where verifiable-reward selection over this particular frozen encoder
has purchase, consistent with evidence that omni models tend to ``read, not listen'' on paralinguistic
cues \citep{voxparadox2026,listenortranscribe2025} and that speaker identity resists in-context
correction \citep{spkverifllm2026}.

\paragraph{Selection is not generation; we did not run a local generative operator.}
Everything we report is \emph{selection}: a frozen bi-encoder reranking a fixed pool of candidate
cards. We never instantiated a locally-driven generative policy that could \emph{produce} candidates
beyond the pool, so we cannot speak to whether generation would recover paralinguistic signal that
selection cannot. The correct attribution of every number here is ``frozen bi-encoder verifiable
selection over candidate cards,'' not best-of-$N$ decoding from a generative model; conflating the two
would overstate what we demonstrated.

\paragraph{Single-corpus, limited-seed intervals.}
The positive result rests on one corpus per task with modest evaluation sets, where bootstrap intervals
\citep{bisani2004bootstrap} are wide and statistical power is low
\citep{card2020power,dror2018hitchhiker,henderson2018drlmatters}. The SLURP corroboration
($0.550\to0.880$, $+0.330$, same frozen-bi-encoder card-selection mechanism) is documented but on a
single split. The negative rests on three (probe) and five (pooling) seeds on CREMA-D alone. Effects
should be read as directional and replicated only within the committed artifacts; any residual mismatch
between an in-text number and its artifact should be read as the artifact governing.

\paragraph{Goodhart pressure, sharpened for a model-dependent reward.}
Pushing the pool size $N$ moves probability mass toward whatever the reward measures, including its
defects \citep{gao2023overopt,skalse2022rewardhacking,rlvrincentive2025}. Verifiable rewards (intent
accuracy, exact match) are more robust than learned reward models but are not immune. The sharper risk
is that our selection reward is a \emph{cosine computed from the same frozen encoder} that produces the
candidate embeddings: it can be high-spread for reasons internal to the model rather than because it
tracks the target skill, so a certified gain may measure the model's self-consistency. Because we
change no weights the failure is recoverable (lower $N$, decouple the verifier), but the reward should
be grounded on held-out, contamination-controlled splits with an externally verifiable target, or the
gain is an artifact of the read-out.

\paragraph{Relation to best-of-$N$ / reranking (honest differentiation).}
The individual ingredients --- best-of-$N$ and reranking \citep{beirami2024bon,verdun2025softbon},
controlled decoding \citep{mudgal2024controlled}, and the Gibbs-optimal tilting
$\qs\propto\qo\exp(\Rwd/\beta)$ from alignment-as-inference \citep{korbak2022kl,yang2024asymptotics} ---
are all public, and our positive mechanism is close to that territory. We do not claim the mechanism as
novel. What is specific here is the coupling of a \emph{frozen omni-embed encoder} to spoken-language
\emph{intent/SLU selection}, together with a precise committed \emph{paralinguistic negative} and the
reward-spread lens that explains the contrast --- not ASR best-of-$N$, which is well-trodden elsewhere.
The contribution is integrative and empirical, and should be weighted as such.

\paragraph{Future work: the agent-level program is deferred, not disproved.}
A larger cross-session paralinguistic-memory program --- one frozen model routing and rewarding another
across sessions --- does not follow from what we ran, and we defer it honestly. It would require three
things we do not have: (a) a cross-session, same-speaker corpus (absent from our frozen data), on which
paralinguistic memory could even be measured; (b) a locally-driven generative operator, so that
candidates are produced rather than merely selected; and (c) a genuinely new non-separable
irreducibility theorem showing that decomposing a task into isolated contexts can add optimization
headroom a single model lacks. Absent (c), the spread lens is explicit that context isolation adds
\emph{nothing} on a fixed reward: with no new non-constant reward component there is no new spread, and
by the identity below no new gain. Establishing that such a component exists, and that it carries
spread, is the precondition for any agent-level claim --- and it is exactly what our present data do not
supply.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to test one modest, falsifiable claim on a single frozen speech model, changing no weights
and no structure: that the gain available to training-free RL --- realised here as inference-time
\emph{verifiable-reward selection}, a frozen bi-encoder ranking candidate cards by cosine --- is
governed by the \emph{reward spread} across candidates, and not by any agentic machinery. On the
content/intent side the spread is real and large, and selection turns it into gain: on MInDS-14 en-US
tool/intent the frozen bi-encoder reaches Acc@1 $0.984$ against a raw-schema baseline of $0.857$, a
paired improvement of $+0.126$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.077,0.181]$, $n=182$, seed $42$;
\texttt{\_repro/minds14\_toolintent\_paired.json}), with SLURP intent corroborating
($0.550\to0.880$, $+0.330$) through the same mechanism. On the paralinguistic side the spread is
essentially absent on this read-out: the frozen content encoder probes near chance for speaker
(mean $0.033$, $3.0\times$ chance) and only modestly for emotion ($0.405$, $2.4\times$ chance) on
CREMA-D, and pool-selection yields no significant emotion gain (across-seed CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$). We
report the negative not as a setback but as a result of equal standing: a precise, committed
measurement that scopes exactly where the method works.

The lens that ties these together is deliberately lightweight and used qualitatively only. The identity
$\gain=\beta\,\KL{\qo}{\qs}$ --- the free-energy / Donsker--Varadhan characterisation of the gap between
what the frozen model already does ($\qo$) and what the reward would have it do ($\qs$) --- is a known
result, not a novel theorem, and we invoke it to \emph{explain}, not to prove: the gain is bounded by
the reward spread over $\mathrm{supp}(\qo)$, so a flat reward yields vanishing gain while a high-spread
reward yields large gain, on the very same frozen model. Two strict, sorry-free Lean lemmas anchor the
qualitative core: a flat reward forces zero gain (\texttt{flat\_no\_gain}), and a non-constant reward
forces strictly positive gain (\texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}). That is precisely the contrast we
observe --- content/intent has spread and selection helps; paralinguistics, on this read-out, has
little spread and it does not.

The conclusion is therefore modest and, we believe, true. Training-free verifiable-reward selection on
one frozen speech model helps for high-spread content and intent, does not help for low-spread
paralinguistics on the content read-out we measured, and the reward-spread lens explains why. What we
offer is a small, reproducible pair of results and a lens that predicts their sign --- not a deployable
agentic stack, and not a claim beyond the single model and the committed artifacts we can currently
stand behind.
