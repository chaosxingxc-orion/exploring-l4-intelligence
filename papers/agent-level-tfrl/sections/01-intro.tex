\begin{abstract}
We study \emph{weight-frozen reward-guided inference-time optimization} on \emph{frozen omni speech models} --- which we abbreviate \textbf{TFRL}, this project's standing shorthand for what is loosely called ``training-free RL'': reward-guided optimization applied purely at inference time, with no weight updates and no architecture change. We are careful to distinguish TFRL from \emph{test-time RL} (TTRL) in the literature \citep{zuo2025ttrl}: TTRL-style methods still adapt via a self-supervised objective computed at test time, whereas TFRL as used throughout this paper changes no parameters whatsoever --- only the inference-time selection distribution changes. Our primary result is a \emph{genuine} reward-driven best-of-$N$ on a frozen \emph{generative} omni model. On Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (Q8\_0 GGUF, driven by llama.cpp), held fixed, we sample $N$ candidate transcripts per utterance and select with a verifiable reward---best-of-$N$ over model-sampled candidates. We state the theoretical relation precisely: hard best-of-$N$ induces the \emph{order-statistics selection distribution} over the sampled pool, a distinct object from the Gibbs tilt $\qs\propto\qo\exp(\Rwd/\beta)$, which is realized by \emph{soft} (temperature-controlled) best-of-$N$ rather than by the hard argmax we run; no operator-linked machine proof connects our Lean lemmas to the hard-BoN selector, and the Beirami KL bound on the order-statistics object is imported into Lean as a named axiom, not derived. On a clean, seed-separated redo of LibriSpeech test-other (cohort seed, noise seed, greedy temperature $0$, and $3$ pool seeds all varied independently), corpus-level greedy WER is $0.0973$ under additive noise (snr5) and $0.0579$ clean; the reference-based oracle selector (an upper bound, not deployable, since it consults the ground truth at selection time) realizes significant corpus-WER headroom at $N=8$ in both conditions---$+0.0336$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0235,0.0453]$, snr5) and $+0.0223$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0136,0.0316]$, clean). Among deployable, label-free selectors, MBR-consensus (corrected to a symmetric edit-distance implementation) is positive but non-significant at $N=8$ in both conditions; logprob-confidence is the sole deployable selector whose corpus-WER CI excludes $0$ in both conditions ($+0.0081$ $[0.0005,0.0161]$ snr5, corpus-only; $+0.0094$ $[0.0034,0.0165]$ clean, both metrics), realizing roughly $24\%$/$42\%$ of the oracle headroom---a Stage-1 directional finding, multiplicity-uncorrected. We read this through a lightweight lens: the gain of selection is $\gain=\beta\KL{\qo}{\qs}$, bounded by the \emph{reward spread} over the model's sampled candidates, the Donsker--Varadhan / free-energy identity used \emph{qualitatively}, with two strict sorry-free Lean~4 lemmas (a flat reward yields zero gain, \texttt{flat\_no\_gain}; a non-constant reward yields strictly positive gain, \texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}). Separately, we \emph{probe}---not RL---a frozen content bi-encoder (omni-embed-nemotron-3b): its embeddings carry near-chance speaker identity (mean acc $0.033$, $3.0\times$ chance, $91$-way) and only modest emotion ($0.405$, $2.4\times$ chance, $6$-way) on CREMA-D, and a TFRL read-out configuration search (layer/pooling, not selection over model-sampled candidates) yields no significant emotion gain ($95\%$ CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$). On MInDS-14 tool/intent identification, a clean factorial separating instruction wording from candidate-card content shows the true zero-shot condition (instruction only, no examples) \emph{regresses} $-0.245$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.286,-0.201]$) relative to a naive baseline; the previously reported gain was driven almost entirely by in-set example cards ($+0.246$), with the pure instruction-on-cards effect only $+0.015$---neither zero-shot nor a reward-guided-RL result. We state the caveats plainly: llama.cpp audio input is experimental upstream; snr5 is a robustness perturbation chosen for measurable headroom; the oracle uses the reference and is an upper bound, not deployable. All results are on frozen models with committed reproduction artifacts, internally re-verified by adversarial AI agents (not an independent team).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

We ask how much can be extracted from \emph{frozen omni speech models} without touching a weight or changing their structure, using reward-guided optimization applied purely at inference time. A modern omni or speech-capable multimodal LLM is, by release, an extraordinary repository of latent capability: from large-scale audio, parallel speech-text corpora, and instruction mixtures, models such as Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen3-Omni, and SALMONN \citep{qwen2audio,qwen25omni,qwen3omni,salmonn2023} acquire transcription, translation, speaker identity, paralinguistic affect, and dialogue competence simultaneously. Our question is deliberately conservative: \emph{how far can one go with a model held fixed?} We treat TFRL (weight-frozen reward-guided inference-time optimization, our abbreviation for what is loosely called ``training-free RL'') concretely as inference-time \emph{verifiable-reward selection}---above all best-of-$N$ over candidates the model itself samples---that changes no parameters and no architecture \citep{beirami2024bon,mudgal2024controlled}. This is distinct from \emph{test-time RL} (TTRL) in the literature \citep{zuo2025ttrl}, which optimizes the policy itself via an actual training step at test time; we do not run anything of that kind. Our primary result realises TFRL genuinely: a frozen \emph{generative} omni model produces $N$ transcript candidates per utterance, and a verifiable reward selects among them. A second study is honest \emph{probing} of a frozen content encoder---a distinct operator that we are careful \emph{not} to dress up as reward-driven selection. Every result below is on a frozen model; no weights change anywhere.

\paragraph{The lever is the reward spread.}
The premise that organizes this paper is that the gain available to inference-time selection is a property of \emph{which reward, over which candidates} one selects. When the reward is nearly \emph{flat} over the candidate set the frozen model actually places mass on, selection moves through a narrow envelope of near-identical rewards and cannot manufacture headroom the candidate distribution does not contain. When the model's samples \emph{spread} in reward, selection can climb, and the headroom grows as more candidates are drawn. For speech in particular, a generative omni model under acoustic stress samples transcripts of genuinely different quality---so a verifiable transcription reward has spread to exploit---whereas a frozen content encoder tends to ``read'' a transcribable surface rather than ``listen'' to the acoustic substrate, exposing spread for content and intent but little for paralinguistics \citep{min2022rethinking,voxparadox2026,hsu2023iclspeech}. The central object of the paper is this reward \emph{spread}, and the empirical fact that it is large for the model's noisy transcript candidates and small for paralinguistic read-out.

\paragraph{Selection as Gibbs tilting.}
To reason about this quantitatively we adopt the standard variational view of inference-time alignment. Optimizing the KL-regularized objective
\[
\Fobj{\qz} \;=\; \Exp_{z\sim \qz}[\Rwd(z)] \;-\; \beta\,\KL{\qz}{\qo}
\]
over selection distributions $\qz$ on the candidate space $\cZ$ has the closed-form Gibbs optimum $\qs(z)\propto \qo(z)\exp(\Rwd(z)/\beta)$, with partition function $\Zpart$ \citep{korbak2022kl,beirami2024bon}. Soft best-of-$N$ and other temperature-controlled refinements \citep{verdun2025softbon,yang2024asymptotics,gui2024bonbon}, self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}, and MBR/reranking \citep{ichihara2025mbr,mbrasr2025} are estimators of, or procedures approximating, this tilt over some realization of $\cZ$. We are precise about where \emph{hard} best-of-$N$---the argmax over $N$ i.i.d.\ draws from $\qo$ that we run empirically---sits relative to this object: hard BoN induces the \emph{order-statistics selection distribution} over the sampled pool, a distinct distribution from $\qs$; its relation to the $\beta\!\to\!0$ limit of the tilt is the standard heuristic correspondence in the literature, not a proven identity in this paper (the Beirami KL bound \citep{beirami2024bon} characterizes the order-statistics object itself, and \emph{soft} BoN, not hard BoN, is what actually realizes $\qs$ at finite $\beta$). Taking $\qo$ to be the frozen generative model's sampling distribution, reward-argmax selection's attainable benefit is nonetheless bounded by how far a reward-sharpened distribution can depart from $\qo$---governed by the spread of $\Rwd$ over $\operatorname{supp}(\qo)$, i.e.\ over the candidates the model actually draws; this bound is what our reward-spread lens formalizes below, and it does not depend on whether the hard-BoN/Gibbs correspondence is exact.

\paragraph{A reward-spread lens.}
Our lens is a single, lightweight identity, stated at its true strength rather than as a discovery. Granted the Gibbs framing, the gain is exactly $\gain=\Fobj{\qs}-\Fobj{\qo}=\beta\KL{\qo}{\qs}$, and a Hoeffding argument caps it by the reward spread, $\gain\le\spread^2/(8\beta)$; to leading order the discriminator sharpens to $\gain\approx\mathrm{Var}_{\qo}(\Rwd)/(2\beta)$. This is the Donsker--Varadhan / free-energy identity, and we are explicit that we use it \emph{qualitatively}, not as a novel theorem. Its two load-bearing corners are machine-checked sorry-free in Lean~4: a flat reward yields zero gain (\texttt{flat\_no\_gain}), and a non-constant reward yields strictly positive gain (\texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}). The reading is direct, and it speaks to the \emph{sign and ceiling} of our primary experiment: flat reward across the model's sampled transcripts $\Rightarrow$ no best-of-$N$ headroom; spread across them $\Rightarrow$ strictly-positive headroom, bounded by that spread. The \emph{growth} of realized headroom with $N$ is a separate, order-statistics effect ($\Exp[\max_{i\le N}\Rwd]$ nondecreasing in $N$), not a consequence of the identity.

\paragraph{Relation to prior training-free work.}
Training-free selection---best-of-$N$, reranking, MBR---is established, most visibly for ASR and open-ended generation \citep{beirami2024bon,mbrasr2025,ichihara2025mbr}. We do \emph{not} claim novelty in the best-of-$N$ mechanism itself. Our contribution is threefold and honestly demarcated: (i) a genuine reward-driven best-of-$N$ run on a \emph{frozen generative omni} model under acoustic stress, with a significant, $N$-scaling oracle headroom and an honest realized-versus-headroom gap for the deployable selector; (ii) a precise \emph{probing} study of a frozen content encoder, reported as probing rather than RL; and (iii) the reward-spread lens that ties the two together. The encoder study and the generative best-of-$N$ are \emph{distinct operators}; we do not claim identical machinery between them.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make exactly three.

\textbf{(C1) A genuine reward-driven best-of-$N$ on a frozen generative omni model.} On Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (Q8\_0 GGUF via llama.cpp), held fixed, we sample $N$ candidate transcripts per utterance and select with a verifiable reward: genuine best-of-$N$ over model-sampled candidates, which induces the \emph{order-statistics selection distribution} over the pool (a heuristic, not proven, correspondence to the Gibbs tilt at $\beta\!\to\!0$; \Cref{sec:osa}). On LibriSpeech test-other, with four seed factors independently varied (cohort seed $20260711$; noise seed $20260712$; greedy temperature $0$, seed inert; $3$ pool seeds; pool $=8$, temperature $0.8$; $n=96$ fixed cohort $\times$ 3 pool seeds), we evaluate two conditions: additive noise (snr5) and clean. Corpus-level greedy WER is $0.0973$ (snr5) and $0.0579$ (clean). The oracle-WER best-of-$N$---the verifiable-reward argmax, an \emph{upper bound}, not deployable---reduces corpus WER by $+0.0336$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0235,0.0453]$, significant, snr5) and $+0.0223$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0136,0.0316]$, significant, clean) at $N=8$. Among deployable, label-free selectors, MBR-consensus (corrected to a symmetric edit-distance implementation; the earlier version's asymmetric formula was a bug) is positive but non-significant at $N=8$ in both conditions; logprob-confidence is the sole deployable selector whose corpus-WER CI excludes $0$ in both conditions---$+0.0081$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0005,0.0161]$, corpus-only, snr5) and $+0.0094$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0034,0.0165]$, significant on both corpus and macro, clean)---realizing roughly $24\%$ / $42\%$ of the oracle headroom (Stage-1 directional, multiplicity-uncorrected). Committed artifacts \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_snr5.json} and \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_clean.json}, with the reproduction command committed. Caveats stated plainly: llama.cpp audio input is experimental upstream (``may have reduced quality''); snr5 additive noise is a robustness perturbation chosen to expose headroom; the oracle uses the reference (an upper bound, not deployable), while logprob-confidence is currently the strongest deployable proxy.

\textbf{(C2) A frozen-encoder probe (not RL, a distinct operator).} We \emph{probe}---we do not run reward-driven selection on---a frozen content bi-encoder, omni-embed-nemotron-3b. On CREMA-D (dev-selected layer, $3$ seeds; committed \texttt{\_repro/paralinguistic\_negative\_probe.json}) its frozen embeddings carry near-chance speaker identity (mean acc $0.033$, $3.0\times$ chance, $91$-way) and only modest emotion ($0.405$, $2.4\times$ chance, $6$-way); TFRL pool selection yields \emph{no} significant emotion gain (across-seed $95\%$ CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$, spanning zero; committed \texttt{\_repro/emotion\_pool\_paired\_v2.json}). Honestly noted, and \emph{never} described as zero-shot or as a reward-guided-RL result: the same encoder also does tool/intent classification by candidate-card cosine retrieval on MInDS-14 en-US. A clean factorial (disjoint card-pool/eval split, disjoint support draws across three seeds; committed \texttt{\_repro/minds14\_toolintent\_v2.json}) separates instruction wording from card content: the true zero-shot condition (task instruction only, no examples) \emph{regresses} to $\mathrm{Acc@1}=0.467$ from a $0.712$ naive baseline ($-0.245$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.286,-0.201]$, significant); the gain instead comes almost entirely from in-set example cards ($+0.246$ over naive, cards alone), with the pure instruction-on-cards effect only $+0.015$. Its verifiable number is only the \emph{evaluation} metric---it does not drive selection---so it is a distinct operator from C1's generative best-of-$N$, and the earlier ``+0.126 zero-shot policy gain'' framing is retired.

\textbf{(C3) A lightweight reward-spread lens.} We formalize the gain identity $\gain=\beta\KL{\qo}{\qs}$ and its spread ceiling, use it qualitatively as the Donsker--Varadhan / free-energy identity, and support it with two strict, sorry-free Lean~4 lemmas (\texttt{flat\_no\_gain}, \texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}). The lens explains the \emph{sign and ceiling} of C1: by the identity and the two lemmas, a reward with genuine spread across the model's sampled candidates yields strictly-positive best-of-$N$ headroom (a flat reward yields none), bounded by the reward spread. It does \emph{not} predict the $N$-curve---that monotone climb is an order-statistics effect ($\Exp[\max_{i\le N}\Rwd]$ nondecreasing in $N$), which we attribute as such rather than to the lens. It also explains the C2 contrast qualitatively: the frozen content encoder exposes reward spread for content/intent but little for paralinguistics.

\paragraph{Scope and honest framing.}
C1 is a genuine but \emph{modest} training-free RL result: a significant, $N$-climbing oracle headroom, but a non-significant deployable selector, on an experimental audio path under a chosen robustness perturbation. C2 is honest probing of a frozen encoder, not RL, and a distinct operator. C3 is a lens, not a discovery. All results are on frozen models with committed reproduction artifacts; we under-claim deliberately.

\paragraph{Organization.}
\Cref{sec:intro} has framed the reward-spread lens and the three contributions. The remainder develops the Gibbs-tilt objective and the spread identity, with the two sorry-free Lean lemmas; presents the frozen generative best-of-$N$ setup and the LibriSpeech noisy-ASR results (C1); reports the frozen-encoder probe---the CREMA-D paralinguistic negative and the corrected MInDS-14 instruction/card factorial (C2); and closes with related work, limitations, and future directions.
