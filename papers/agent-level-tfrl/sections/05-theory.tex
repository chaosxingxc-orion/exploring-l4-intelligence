\section{A Reward-Spread Lens}
\label{sec:osa}

We keep one lightweight analytic tool, used \emph{qualitatively} to organize the empirical
contrast between content/intent and paralinguistics --- not as a novel theorem, and not as a
``machine-checked quantitative ceiling.'' The claim is simple: inference-time verifiable-reward
selection over a frozen model can only realize gain to the extent that the reward actually
\emph{spreads} across the candidates it ranks. Where the reward has spread (content, tool/intent),
selection helps; where it is nearly flat (speaker, emotion on our frozen omni read-out), it cannot.

Fix a finite candidate set $\cZ$ (the candidate cards a frozen bi-encoder ranks), a strictly
positive base measure $\qo:\cZ\to\R_{>0}$ with $\sum_{w\in\cZ}\qo(w)=1$, a bounded verifiable reward
$\Rwd:\cZ\to\R$, and an inverse-temperature $\beta>0$. Write $\Exp_{\qz}[\cdot]$ for expectation
under $\qz$, adopt $0\log0=0$, and define the tilted (Gibbs) target and its normalizer
\[
\Zpart \;:=\; \Exp_{\qo}\!\big[\exp(\Rwd/\beta)\big] \;>\;0,
\qquad
\qs(z) \;:=\; \frac{\qo(z)\,\exp\!\big(\Rwd(z)/\beta\big)}{\Zpart},
\]
together with the KL-regularized objective
$\Fobj{\qz} := \Exp_{\qz}[\Rwd] - \beta\,\KL{\qz}{\qo}$. This is the standard inference-time
specialization of KL-regularized reward maximization, whose maximizer is the exponential tilt
\citep{korbak2022kl,mudgal2024controlled}.

\paragraph{Master identity: gain is a KL divergence.}
A one-line algebraic identity (substitute $\Rwd(z)=\beta(\log\tfrac{\qs(z)}{\qo(z)}+\log\Zpart)$ into
$\Fobj{\cdot}$) gives, for every distribution $\qz$ on $\cZ$,
\[
\Fobj{\qz} \;=\; \beta\log\Zpart \;-\; \beta\,\KL{\qz}{\qs}.
\]
Reading it at the model's own distribution $\qz=\qo$ and defining the optimization \emph{gain}
$\gain := \Fobj{\qs}-\Fobj{\qo}$ yields the object we actually care about:
\[
\gain \;=\; \beta\,\KL{\qo}{\qs}
\;=\; \beta\log\Exp_{\qo}\!\big[\exp(\Rwd/\beta)\big] - \Exp_{\qo}[\Rwd].
\tag{$\star$}\label{eq:gain}
\]
The gain is exactly the divergence between what the model already does, $\qo$, and what the reward
would have it do, $\qs$. The right-hand form of \eqref{eq:gain} is the free-energy / cumulant
expression $\beta\log\Exp_{\qo}[e^{\Rwd/\beta}]-\Exp_{\qo}[\Rwd]$; by the Donsker--Varadhan variational
identity it is nonnegative, and it is governed by how much $\Rwd$ varies under $\qo$. We use this
\emph{qualitatively}: a second-order expansion gives $\gain \approx \operatorname{Var}_{\qo}(\Rwd)/(2\beta)$
for a concentrated $\qo$, so the gain scales with the \emph{spread} $\spread$ of the reward across
candidates. This is a well-known identity, invoked here as an organizing lens, not billed as a new
result or a tight ceiling.

\paragraph{Two endpoints (both sorry-free in Lean).}
The lens has two exact endpoints, which we have formalized in Lean~4 without \texttt{sorry}.

\emph{(i) Flat reward $\Rightarrow$ zero gain} (\texttt{flat\_no\_gain}). If $\Rwd\equiv c$ is constant
on $\cZ$, then $\Zpart=e^{c/\beta}$, so $\qs=\qo$ and, by \eqref{eq:gain}, $\gain=0$. A reward that
cannot distinguish the candidates leaves the model unchanged: there is nothing for selection to
exploit.

\emph{(ii) Non-constant reward $\Rightarrow$ strictly positive gain} (\texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}).
If $\Rwd$ is non-constant on $\cZ$ (recall $\qo>0$ everywhere), then $\qo\ne\qs$: were they equal,
$e^{\Rwd(z)/\beta}=\Zpart$ for all $z$ would force $\Rwd$ constant. By the strict Gibbs inequality
$\KL{\qo}{\qs}>0$, so \eqref{eq:gain} gives $\gain>0$. Any genuine spread in the verifiable reward
yields strictly positive headroom for selection to realize.

These two lemmas are the strict converse of one another: $\Rwd$ constant $\iff \qs=\qo \iff \gain=0$.

\paragraph{Why the lens explains our results.}
The lens reads directly onto the empirical contrast of this paper. For \emph{content and intent}, the
verifiable reward separates candidate cards sharply: on MInDS-14 en-US tool/intent, frozen bi-encoder
candidate-card selection reaches $\mathrm{Acc}@1\,{=}\,0.984$ versus the raw-schema baseline $0.857$
($\Delta=+0.126$; \Cref{tab:content}), and SLURP intent corroborates ($0.550\to0.880$). Here $\Rwd$ has
high spread across candidates, so by \eqref{eq:gain} and endpoint (ii) selection realizes a real gain
--- the inference-time analogue of why best-of-$N$ and reranking pay off in proportion to how much a
verifiable reward separates candidates \citep{beirami2024bon,gao2023overopt}. For \emph{paralinguistics},
the picture is the opposite. The frozen omni content read-out carries near-chance speaker information
and only modest emotion (\Cref{tab:para}), and training-free pool-selection produces no
significant emotion gain (across-seed $95\%$ CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$; committed artifact
\texttt{\_repro/emotion\_pool\_paired\_v2.json}). On these factors $\Rwd$ is nearly flat on
$\mathrm{supp}(\qo)$, so by endpoint (i) there is little spread to exploit and selection cannot help
--- exactly what we observe. The reward-spread lens thus does one job, and only that job: it explains
why identical training-free selection machinery succeeds on content/intent and fails on
paralinguistics, without appeal to model class, decomposition, or agentic structure.
