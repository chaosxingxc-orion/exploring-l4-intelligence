\section{Empirical Results}
\label{sec:feasibility}

We report two results on frozen omni speech \emph{models}, each obtained on a single frozen model with \emph{no weight updates and no structural change}. The primary result is genuine training-free RL: on a frozen \emph{generative} omni model we sample $N$ candidate transcripts per utterance and select among them with a verifiable reward---reward-driven best-of-$N$ over model-sampled candidates, on a frozen model (\Cref{sec:res:bon}). The secondary result is honest \emph{probing} of a different frozen model, a content bi-encoder: a paralinguistic read-out and a null emotion-selection gain, with an accompanying zero-shot-classification ablation (\Cref{sec:res:probe}). The two are read through a single quantitative lens---the \emph{spread} of the verifiable reward across candidates---which we state first (\Cref{sec:res:lens}). We are careful to keep the operators distinct: the primary result is reward-driven selection over \emph{generated} candidates; the encoder probe is not reward-driven selection at all, and we make no claim of shared machinery between them.

\subsection{The Reward-Spread Lens}
\label{sec:res:lens}

The optimization performed by \emph{soft}, temperature-controlled selection is the KL-regularised tilt of a frozen base distribution $\qo$ toward a verifiable reward $\Rwd$: maximising $\Fobj(q)=\Exp_{q}[\Rwd]-\beta\,\KL(q\,\|\,\qo)$ has optimum $\qs \propto \qo\,\exp(\Rwd/\beta)$, the object that underwrites reward-guided decoding \citep{korbak2022kl,mudgal2024controlled,beirami2024bon}. As $\beta\!\to\!0$ this tilt concentrates on the highest-reward candidate. The \emph{hard} best-of-$N$ oracle we run empirically induces the order-statistics selection distribution over its sampled pool---the standard heuristic identifies this with the $\beta\to0$ limit above, and we use that heuristic to organize the discussion below, without treating it as a proven identity (\Cref{sec:osa}, \Cref{app:lean}). The gain of the tilt admits the identity
\[
  \gain \;=\; \beta\,\KL(\qo\,\|\,\qs),
\]
which we use \emph{qualitatively}: it is the Donsker--Varadhan / free-energy identity for the log-partition function $\log\Zpart=\log\Exp_{\qo}[\exp(\Rwd/\beta)]$, not a novel theorem of ours. Its content for us is a comparison, not a constant: the gain is controlled by the reward \emph{spread} $\spread=\max\Rwd-\min\Rwd$ over the candidates in $\mathrm{supp}(\qo)$. A flat reward---one that does not discriminate among candidates---yields \emph{zero} gain; a reward with genuine spread across the model's sampled candidates yields \emph{strictly positive} gain, and does so on a single frozen model, with no decomposition or additional structure required. Two strict, \texttt{sorry}-free Lean lemmas make exactly these two endpoints precise: \texttt{flat\_no\_gain} (a constant reward gives $\gain=0$) and \texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant} (a non-constant reward gives $\gain>0$). We claim nothing beyond these two facts; they suffice to organise the evidence.

The lens speaks directly to the primary result: the oracle best-of-$N$ headroom is governed by the spread of the verifiable reward across the model's sampled candidates. When the candidates agree (little spread), there is no headroom; when they disagree in a way the reward can score (spread), there is headroom, bounded by the (\,$N$-independent\,) reward spread; the realized oracle best-of-$N$ climbs toward that fixed ceiling as $N$ grows purely by order statistics ($\Exp[\max_{i\le N}\Rwd]$ nondecreasing in $N$), not because the spread itself enlarges. The same lens frames the secondary contrast qualitatively: the frozen content encoder exposes reward spread for content and intent, but little for paralinguistics, so a probe over it finds structure in the former and near-none in the latter.

\subsection{Genuine Reward-Guided Best-of-$N$ on a Frozen Omni Model}
\label{sec:res:bon}\label{sec:c1}

Our primary result is genuine TFRL: reward-driven selection over candidates that a frozen generative model actually samples, which induces the \emph{order-statistics selection distribution} over the sampled pool (\Cref{sec:osa}; its relation to the Gibbs tilt at $\beta\!\to\!0$ is a heuristic correspondence, not a proven identity). On \textbf{Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B} (Q8\_0 GGUF, driven by \texttt{llama.cpp}) we sample $N$ candidate transcripts per utterance and select one under a verifiable reward---no weights are touched and no structure is added. We evaluate on LibriSpeech \texttt{test-other} under two conditions: additive noise at $\mathrm{SNR}{=}5$\,dB (a robustness perturbation chosen because it leaves measurable headroom over greedy decoding) and clean; sampling pool $8$, temperature $0.8$.

This is a clean-protocol redo that supersedes an earlier run whose ``three generation seeds'' conflated several sources of randomness into one label. We separate four factors that must not be pooled into a single ``seed'': a \emph{cohort} draw (a fixed $n{=}96$-utterance sample per condition, cohort seed $20260711$); a \emph{noise realization} (noise seed $20260712$, snr5 only, one draw per utterance); the \emph{greedy} decode (temperature $0$, so its seed is inert by construction); and $3$ independently-varied \emph{pool-generation} seeds $\{1,2,3\}$ that resample the $N{=}8$ candidate pool per utterance. Only the pool-generation seeds are genuine independent replicates of one factor; the hierarchical (utterance-then-pool-seed) bootstrap CIs below resample exactly that structure, holding cohort and noise fixed (committed artifacts \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_snr5.json}, \texttt{\_repro/asr\_bon\_v2\_clean.json}).

We report results on the \emph{corpus} WER metric (total edit distance over total reference words); macro (per-utterance-averaged) utterance-WER figures are given only where explicitly labeled as such, since the two are not interchangeable and conflating them was part of the original error. Five selectors are evaluated: the \emph{oracle-WER} selector takes the pool candidate of minimum WER against the reference---the verifiable-reward argmax, an \emph{upper bound}, not deployable, because it consults the reference. Among deployable, label-free selectors: \emph{MBR-consensus} selects the candidate of minimum expected edit-distance risk against the rest of the pool (here using a corrected \emph{symmetric} edit-distance implementation; the prior version's risk formula was asymmetric, a bug); \emph{logprob-confidence} selects the candidate of highest mean token log-probability; \emph{random} and \emph{length} (shortest-hypothesis) are sanity-check controls. \Cref{tab:bon} reports oracle, MBR, and logprob-confidence on the corpus metric across $N$ in both conditions.

The verifiable-reward headroom is real, significant, and now replicates across two conditions. Corpus-level greedy WER is $0.0973$ (snr5) and $0.0579$ (clean). Oracle best-of-$N$ at $N{=}8$ reduces corpus WER by $+0.0336$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0235,0.0453]$, significant, snr5) and $+0.0223$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0136,0.0316]$, significant, clean), climbing with $N$ in both conditions (\Cref{tab:bon}). For readers who want the macro (per-utterance-averaged) \emph{macro utterance-WER} figure, labeled explicitly as such: oracle-8 macro-WER delta is $+0.0353$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0234,0.0500]$, snr5) and $+0.0254$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0133,0.0397]$, clean)---consistent in sign and significance with the corpus figures, but not the same statistic, and never presented as the unqualified primary WER number. In both conditions a single temperature-$0.8$ sample at $N{=}1$ is no better than greedy, and on clean it is a \emph{significant regression} ($-0.0100$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0185,-0.0022]$)---an honest new observation the seed-separated protocol surfaces. We attribute the sign/ceiling of the headroom and its growth with $N$ to two distinct causes and do not conflate them: the reward-spread lens (\Cref{sec:osa}) accounts for the \emph{sign and ceiling}, while the \emph{growth with $N$} is a separate order-statistics effect of the order-statistics selection distribution the hard-BoN oracle induces (\Cref{sec:osa,app:lean}).

We are equally plain about the realized-vs-headroom gap, and about a genuinely new finding. MBR-consensus (corrected to a symmetric edit-distance implementation) is positive but non-significant at $N{=}8$ in both conditions ($+0.0048$ corpus, snr5; $+0.0021$ corpus, clean). Under the superseded v1 data (whose asymmetric MBR-risk formula was itself part of the original error, and whose seeds conflated cohort/noise/pool), a separate review found MBR significantly \emph{worse} than greedy at $N{=}1$/$N{=}2$ on the corpus metric; that finding motivated the symmetric-distance fix applied here and is not reproduced as stated once cohort/noise/pool are cleanly separated. The genuinely new verified result is that \emph{logprob-confidence} is the sole deployable selector whose corpus-WER CI excludes $0$ in \emph{both} conditions at $N{=}8$: $+0.0081$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0005,0.0161]$, corpus-only, snr5) and $+0.0094$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.0034,0.0165]$, significant on both corpus and macro, clean)---realizing roughly $24\%$ and $42\%$ of the respective oracle headroom. We flag this as \textbf{Stage-1 directional}: the snr5 lower CI bound is marginal ($\approx5\times10^{-4}$), and no correction for multiple comparisons (five selectors $\times$ four $N$ $\times$ two conditions $\times$ two metrics) has been applied. The honest reading is unchanged in kind: the reward-defined oracle headroom is significant and climbs with $N$ in both conditions; among deployable selectors, only logprob-confidence currently converts a significant fraction of it into a significant gain, and only under this uncorrected, single-runtime, single-noise-realization protocol.

Caveats we state rather than bury. (i) \texttt{llama.cpp} audio input is \emph{experimental} upstream and documented as possibly reduced quality; the absolute WERs should be read with that in mind, though the \emph{within-setup} best-of-$N$ contrast is the load-bearing quantity. (ii) snr5 is a deliberate robustness perturbation and clean is exactly that---clean speech---so together they bound rather than eliminate condition-dependence; only one noise realization per utterance is drawn (nested noise replicates are deferred). (iii) The oracle selector uses the reference and is therefore an upper bound, not a deployable system. (iv) Random and length controls are additional deployable baselines: both are significant corpus-metric \emph{regressions} on clean at $N{=}8$ (random $-0.0098$ $[-0.0190,-0.0017]$; length $-0.0426$ $[-0.0571,-0.0290]$), underscoring that a deployable selector can easily make things worse, not just fail to help. Reproduce with the committed command \texttt{scripts/repro\_asr\_best\_of\_n\_v2.py} (with the \texttt{llama-server} resident) against the committed artifacts.

\subsection{Frozen-Encoder Probing: A Paralinguistic Negative}
\label{sec:res:probe}\label{sec:c2}

Our secondary result probes a \emph{different} frozen model and a \emph{different} operator. On a frozen content bi-encoder (\texttt{omni-embed-nemotron-3b}) we analyse what its embeddings carry by probing---not by reward-driven selection. This is not the best-of-$N$ operator of \Cref{sec:res:bon}, and we do not claim shared machinery; it is a measurement of the frozen representation.

First, the frozen content read-out carries little paralinguistic information. On CREMA-D, with the read-out layer selected on dev and three seeds, speaker identity is recoverable at \emph{near chance}---mean accuracy $0.033$ on a $91$-way task ($3.0\times$ chance, chance $0.011$)---and emotion only modestly---mean accuracy $0.405$ on a $6$-way task ($2.4\times$ chance, chance $0.167$) (committed artifact \texttt{\_repro/paralinguistic\_negative\_probe.json}). \Cref{tab:para} summarises. For a \emph{content} embedding, near-chance speaker recovery is close to definitional; an index built on this embedding would retrieve on content, not on who-spoke or how-they-felt.

We keep the scope of the paralinguistic negative tight. It is a linear/kNN probe and a pool-selection experiment on \emph{one} frozen content bi-encoder; it does not generalise to frozen omni models as a class, and CREMA-D content is a $12$-sentence closed read set, so we make no content claim from it. \subsection{Reading the Evidence}
\label{sec:res:reading}

The two results are read through one lens, but they are two different operators on two different frozen models, and we do not conflate them. The primary result is genuine TFRL: reward-driven best-of-$N$ over candidates a frozen generative omni model actually samples---inducing the order-statistics selection distribution, heuristically not provenly linked to the Gibbs tilt (\Cref{sec:osa,app:lean})---where the verifiable-reward headroom is real, significant, and replicates across snr5 and clean ($+0.0336$ $[0.0235,0.0453]$ and $+0.0223$ $[0.0136,0.0316]$ at $N{=}8$, corpus metric), while among deployable label-free selectors only logprob-confidence converts a significant fraction of it into a significant corpus-WER gain in both conditions ($+0.0081$ $[0.0005,0.0161]$ snr5, corpus-only; $+0.0094$ $[0.0034,0.0165]$ clean, both metrics; MBR remains positive but non-significant)---an honest realized-vs-headroom gap, now with one deployable selector that clears it directionally. The secondary result is honest probing of a frozen content encoder: near-chance speaker recovery, only modest emotion, and a null emotion-selection CI $[-0.043,+0.116]$; the encoder's tool/intent classification on MInDS-14 is, once instruction and card content are cleanly separated, a card-representation result whose true zero-shot arm \emph{regresses} ($-0.245$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.286,-0.201]$)---never a reward-driven selection gain, and never zero-shot beyond that one arm. The gain identity $\gain=\beta\,\KL(\qo\,\|\,\qs)$ and its two Lean endpoints (\texttt{flat\_no\_gain}, \texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}) organise both qualitatively: the \emph{sign and ceiling} of the best-of-$N$ headroom track the spread of the verifiable reward across the model's sampled candidates (its growth with $N$ is the separate order-statistics effect of the hard-BoN selection distribution), and the frozen content encoder exposes such spread for content and intent but little for paralinguistics. We report every interval in full and state the null as plainly as the positive.
