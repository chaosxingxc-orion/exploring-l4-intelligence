\section{Feasibility Analysis}
\label{sec:feasibility}

We now argue that the agent-level program of this work is not only well-posed but already \emph{instantiable} from off-the-shelf \emph{frozen} components, and that our own preliminary measurements both motivate and sharply \emph{constrain} the design. The argument proceeds in four steps: we identify the two classes of frozen omni models with the two structural roles an episodic agent requires (\Cref{sec:feas:components}); we show that a single verifiable-reward acceptance gate furnishes the control law shared by memory and skills (\Cref{sec:feas:gate}); we state an honest novelty delta against a crowded mechanism literature (\Cref{sec:feas:novelty}); and we present in-house results that locate the operating regime empirically (\Cref{sec:feas:prelim}).

We state at the outset the organising principle that the theory section makes precise and that our measurements bear out. The optimization gain of training-free RL---the KL-regularised tilting $\qs \propto \qo\,\exp(\Rwd/\beta)$---is governed by the \emph{realized reward spread} $\spread$ of the (action-space, reward) pair (OSA-1, Lean-checked). This is a property of the \emph{reward structure}, not of model class. A flat reward (e.g.\ instruction-conditioning of a frozen embedding read-out) gives vanishing gain $\gain$; a high-spread verifiable reward (e.g.\ task-level verifiable selection) gives large gain \emph{even on a single frozen model}. Context-isolated composition makes gains \emph{additive} across blocks (OSA-2, \texttt{gain\_product}), and every non-degenerate block contributes \emph{strictly positive} gain (\texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant}: reward non-constant $\Rightarrow \gain>0$, sorry-free in Lean). But isolation \emph{per se} adds nothing: \texttt{qstar\_product} proves the isolated optimum \emph{equals} the monolithic optimum on the product reward $\Rwd=\sum_i \Rwd_i$. The only new gain comes from introducing additional \emph{non-degenerate} verifiable rewards, and whether a frozen speech model affords non-degenerate per-block rewards for a given factor is an \emph{empirical} question (Conjecture~\ref{conj:spreadfloor}, the per-block spread floor), not a theorem. Our preliminary results answer it affirmatively for content/intent and negatively---or at best fragilely---for paralinguistics, and we report both plainly.

\subsection{The Two Omni Classes as Agent Components}
\label{sec:feas:components}

The decisive enabling observation is that the contemporary omni-model ecosystem ships, off the shelf, the two computational primitives an episodic agent needs, and that both can be used \emph{frozen}. On one side, \emph{vector-omni bi-encoders} map an audio (or audio--text) input to a fixed embedding in a shared retrieval space \citep{omniembed2025,omniembedaudio2026}; their natural role is the agent's \emph{memory and index}, the read/write substrate over past episodes, as speech-native retrieval-augmented systems already use them \citep{wavrag2025,speechrag2024,voxrag2025}. On the other side, \emph{generative thinker--talker} omni models map an input to a token sequence under a decoding policy \citep{qwen3omni,qwen25omni,salmonn2023}; their natural role is the agent's \emph{policy and skill executor}. Both classes are pretrained, publicly released, and, for our purposes, \emph{frozen}: the action lives entirely in the inference-time degrees of freedom $\cZ$, partitioned into the embedding/retrieval operator $\cZA$ realised by the bi-encoder and the generative operator $\cZB$ realised by the thinker--talker. This separation lets us treat memory construction (Operator-A) and skill execution (Operator-B) as two faces of a single search distribution $\qz$ over $\cZ$, optimised against the same reward $\Rwd$ without touching weights or architecture.

We must, however, be honest about what the frozen omni \emph{memory} can and cannot index, because our own probes (\Cref{sec:feas:prelim}) constrain it. On the content axis the frozen omni embedding is strong; on the paralinguistic axes it is not. Speaker identity is recoverable at \emph{near chance} from the embedding at every layer ($\approx 0.04$ on a $91$-way task, chance $0.011$), and emotion is recoverable only weakly and fragilely. The two-omni ``vector-omni memory'' therefore does \emph{not} do load-bearing \emph{paralinguistic} retrieval: an index built solely on the omni content embedding would retrieve on content, not on who-spoke or how-they-felt. We accordingly reframe the memory honestly: the frozen omni embedding is reserved for the \emph{content} channel, while paralinguistic memory keys are supplied by classic, frozen, special-purpose speech encoders (an ECAPA-TDNN--style speaker embedder; a dedicated SER read-out). Feasibility thus reduces to whether a \emph{control law} can compose a frozen omni policy with a heterogeneous frozen-encoder memory productively---not to whether the parts can be built, and not to a claim that one omni embedding carries every factor.

\subsection{The Verifiable-Reward Acceptance Gate as the Control Law}
\label{sec:feas:gate}

The control law is a verifiable-reward \emph{acceptance gate}: a candidate memory write or skill invocation is admitted only if it improves a verifiable reward on held-out probes, and is otherwise rejected. This single mechanism governs both faces of $\cZ$. The case for making the gate load-bearing is empirical and, in our reading, strong. It rests substantially on \citet{skillsbench2026}, whose statistics we verified against the source (a $+16.2 average over 6$ tasks in 1$ domains, ranging $+4.5 to $+51.9, with 6/84$ negative), and we triangulate it against the broader agent-stability literature below. \textsc{SkillsBench} reports that \emph{curated} skills raise agent success by $+16.2$ percentage points, whereas \emph{self-generated} skills contribute essentially zero in aggregate, with $16$ of $84$ skills measurably \emph{negative} \citep{skillsbench2026}. The lesson is not that self-evolution is futile but that unfiltered self-evolution is: without an acceptance test, an append-only memory or skill library accrues as much harm as benefit, a failure mode echoed across the agent-stability literature \citep{expfollowing2025,ace2025,remembering2026,reflexion2023}. The gate is precisely the missing filter. When ground-truth labels are scarce, a surrogate verifier can stand in for the reward without collapsing the guarantee, as co-evolved skill--verifier pairs demonstrate \citep{coevoskills2026}; and for speech specifically the verifiable rewards already exist, from paralinguistic-aware speech reward models \citep{gsrm2026,paras2s2025} to the WER/exact-match/intent rewards reused throughout this series. We therefore adopt the gate as the one control law shared by $\cZA$ (admit a memory entry) and $\cZB$ (admit a skill or decoding action), with the surrogate verifier as the fallback when labelled probes run out. This is the same KL-regularised tilting that underwrites best-of-$N$ and reward-guided decoding \citep{korbak2022kl,mudgal2024controlled,beirami2024bon}: maximising $\Fobj(q)=\Exp_{q}[\Rwd]-\beta\,\KL(q\,\|\,\qo)$ with optimum $\qs \propto \qo\,\exp(\Rwd/\beta)$, here lifted from token decisions to episode-level admission decisions. Its gain is exactly the reward-spread quantity of \Cref{sec:feasibility}: a high-spread admission reward makes the gate productive; a flat one makes it inert.

\subsection{Novelty Delta}
\label{sec:feas:novelty}

We state the contribution honestly. The \emph{mechanism}---self-evolving episodic memory plus a growing skill library refined by feedback---is crowded: reflective self-improvement \citep{reflexion2023,expel2023}, library-building agents \citep{voyager2023,awm2024,museautoskill2026}, and gradient-free inference-time credit assignment \citep{jitrl2026} all occupy adjacent ground. Our claim is not the mechanism in the abstract. The unfilled slot is the specific composition: a \emph{frozen omni policy} (Operator-B) and a \emph{frozen-encoder memory} (Operator-A) driven by \emph{episodic self-evolution} under a \emph{verifiable speech reward}, with no weight updates anywhere in the loop. We are explicit that this memory is heterogeneous and \emph{not} a single-vector omni store: as established above and measured below, the frozen omni embedding is at chance on speaker and weak on emotion, so it carries the content channel only, with paralinguistic keys delegated to classic frozen speech encoders. The closest priors fall short on the all-frozen axis. Trainable instruction embedders such as \textsc{bge-en-icl} require gradient training to acquire their in-context behaviour \citep{bgeenicl2024}, violating the frozen-memory constraint; few-shot--emergent audio models such as \textsc{MiMo-Audio} obtain their capability through pretraining rather than through inference-time activation of an already-frozen model \citep{mimoaudio2025}. Neither realises the all-frozen, reward-gated, speech-verifiable agent loop. To our knowledge, this composition has not been demonstrated, and we make the corresponding ``first, to our knowledge'' claim while being explicit that each ingredient in isolation has prior art---and that the load-bearing novelty is the verifiable-reward composition, not any claim that a single omni embedding affords every factor.

\subsection{Preliminary Empirical Evidence}
\label{sec:feas:prelim}

Our in-house measurements locate the operating regime along the single axis the theory identifies: the realized reward spread $\spread$ of each (action-space, reward) pair. We organise the evidence by \emph{channel}. A \textbf{content/intent ``read'' channel}---task-level verifiable selection over a single frozen model---exhibits high spread and large gains. A \textbf{paralinguistic ``listen'' channel}---emotion and speaker read-out from the frozen embedding---exhibits low and fragile spread; for these factors our evidence does \emph{not} support a per-block spread floor. All numbers below are our own preliminary results on a frozen \texttt{omni-embed-nemotron-3b} with kNN probes and bootstrap confidence intervals (CREMA-D dev$600$/test$300$; MInDS-14 dev$280$/test$257$); they are reported verbatim and labelled preliminary. We flag a contamination caveat: CREMA-D content is a $12$-sentence closed read set, so near-ceiling content accuracy ($\approx 1.00$) may partly reflect memorisation of fixed transcripts, and SLURP, MInDS-14 and URO are public benchmarks that may overlap pretraining corpora; the single-model gains below should be read with that risk in mind.

\paragraph{Single-model conditioning: no effect larger than the detectable minimum.}
\Cref{tab:cremad-matrix} reports test probe accuracy for the frozen embedding under four instruction-conditioning regimes, crossed against three read-out factors. The same frozen embedding simultaneously supports near-perfect content read-out ($\approx 1.00$), weak emotion read-out ($\approx 0.36$), and near-chance speaker read-out ($\approx 0.04$; chance on this $91$-way task is $0.011$). Instruction conditioning does not measurably move the factor accuracies: the emotion delta from baseline to emotion-instruction is $+0.007$ with overlapping CIs, the speaker delta is $-0.003$, and the conditioning matrix is not diagonally dominant. We report this with the honesty its statistical power warrants. At $n=300$ the study is \emph{underpowered}; we therefore report it as \emph{no effect larger than the minimum detectable effect (MDE) at $n=300$}, not as a positive ``OSA-1 signature'' and not as a proof that conditioning is exactly inert. A sweep over all $37$ hidden states and two pooling read-outs leaves speaker near chance at \emph{every} layer (full-sequence best $0.043$ at L36; audio-token best $0.043$ at L32), with content saturating early (full-seq $0.990$ at L24) and emotion peaking only weakly (full-seq $0.400$ at L0). Whatever spread instruction-conditioning offers is below what this probe can resolve; we draw no stronger conclusion.

\paragraph{Paralinguistic ``listen'' channel: emotion is fragile and speaker is near chance.}
For the flagship's paralinguistic factors the per-block spread the agent would need is small and not robust. Choosing the read-out itself---an Operator-A ($\cZA$) action over layer and pooling---can extract an emotion gain on \emph{some} splits, but a rigorous multi-seed re-run shows it is not robust, and it forces us to retract an earlier over-claim. We previously reported a single-seed $+0.097$ (attentive-statistics pooling at L16 versus mean-pooling at L0). That figure was the \emph{best seed under oracle TEST-layer selection}---a winner's-curse / selection leak, since the test layer was chosen on the test set. We have since corrected the protocol on three counts: (i) \emph{dev}-layer selection (both the base mean-pool layer and the selected attentive-stats layer are chosen on dev and frozen before test evaluation); (ii) a \emph{paired}-delta bootstrap $95\%$ CI computed directly on the per-example improvement; and (iii) five seeds $\{42,7,123,2024,31337\}$. \Cref{tab:emotion-multiseed} reports the result: mean $\Delta=+0.037$ (across-seed std $0.064$, range $[-0.053,+0.097]$), with the paired CI excluding $0$ in only $2/5$ seeds and two seeds nominally \emph{negative}. The plain reading is that attentive-statistics pooling helps on some splits but is \emph{not robust}, and that the previously reported $+0.097$ is reproduced only as the best of five seeds under the corrected protocol. Speaker read-out is worse: it stays near chance at all $37$ layers ($\approx 0.04$, chance $0.011$), so the frozen embedding encodes essentially no recoverable speaker identity. For paralinguistics, then, our own evidence does \emph{not} support a non-degenerate per-block reward: Conjecture~\ref{conj:spreadfloor} fails or is fragile here. \paragraph{Content/intent ``read'' channel: high spread, large single-model gains.}
The contrast is task-level Operator-B ($\cZB$) selection---choosing among generative candidates of a \emph{single frozen model} under a verifiable reward, the best-of-$N$ training-free-RL move of this series. Here the realized reward spread is large and the gains are well separated \emph{even on a single frozen model} (\Cref{tab:opB}). On SLURP intent, accuracy rises $0.550\!\to\!0.880$ ($+0.330$, CI $[0.288,0.374]$). On MInDS-14 intent, $0.883\!\to\!0.972$ ($+0.089$); a second configuration (raw schema $\to$ policy on the same frozen model) improves $0.852\!\to\!0.984$ ($+0.132$, CI $[0.082,0.187]$). On URO spoken-QA, $0.380\!\to\!0.715$ ($+0.335$, CI $[0.265,0.405]$), with a conservative reranking pass lifting this to $0.845$ at \emph{zero} regressions. We reconcile the provenance honestly. The project's own \texttt{docs/theory.md} reports a \emph{different} pipeline for SLURP ($0.522\!\to\!0.894$, $+0.372$) that subsequently showed a $0.894\!\to\!0.880$ regression under a later change, together with several MInDS variants. We treat that as a \emph{second configuration / pipeline not re-run here}, and report \emph{one reconciled baseline per task} under a fixed selection rule: the lower, conservative baseline$\to$improvement pair measured in our seed-$42$ paired-bootstrap run, with alternative pipelines noted but not treated as canonical. We do \emph{not} present a single clean $+0.330$ as if it were the unique result. What these numbers establish---and all they establish---is that a high-spread verifiable reward yields large gain on a single frozen model, exactly as OSA-1 predicts. We emphasise that this high spread is demonstrated for \textbf{content/intent only}.

\paragraph{Reading the evidence.}
The picture is consistent and, in one direction, humbling. The optimization gain tracks the realized reward spread $\spread$ of the (action-space, reward) pair, \emph{not} the model class: a single frozen model already affords large gains under a high-spread verifiable reward (content/intent: $+0.330$ SLURP, $+0.132$ MInDS, $+0.335$ URO), while a flat reward (instruction-conditioning) affords none we can detect at $n=300$. For content and intent this supports moving the action up to compose verifiable per-block rewards across operators and episodes: by \texttt{gain\_pos\_of\_nonconstant} each non-degenerate block adds strictly positive gain, and by \texttt{gain\_product} these add. But the same theory is honest about its precondition: \texttt{qstar\_product} shows that isolation \emph{per se} buys nothing; the new gain comes only from genuinely non-degenerate per-block rewards. For the flagship's \emph{paralinguistic} factors our evidence does not deliver that precondition---emotion spread is fragile (mean $\Delta=+0.037$, paired CI excludes $0$ in $2/5$ seeds) and speaker is near chance at every layer---so Conjecture~\ref{conj:spreadfloor} fails or is fragile there. We therefore claim the agentic-composition move \emph{for content/intent} and explicitly \emph{decline} to claim it for paralinguistics, where the frozen omni embedding does not afford a load-bearing per-block reward and the omni ``vector memory'' does not perform load-bearing paralinguistic retrieval. We report all intervals, label these numbers preliminary, and state the negative result as plainly as the positive one: the design is motivated where the spread is high and constrained where it is not.
